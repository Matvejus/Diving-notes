
# Required Subject Areas

1. History of Enriched Air Nitrox (EAN). 
2. Physiology:
	1. a. Oxygen. 
	2. b. Nitrogen. 
3. Physics: 
	1. a. Pressure review. 
	2. b. Partial pressures. 
4. Equipment Considerations: 
	1. a. Forty percent oxygen content and less. 
	2. b. Above 40 percent oxygen content. 
5. Dive Tables: 
	1. a. Equivalent air depth (EAD) introduction of concept only for demonstration. 
	2. b. EAN tables.
	3. c. Switching mixes on repetitive dives.
6. Dive Computers: 
	1. a. Mix adjustable. 
	2. b. Oxygen (O₂) integrated. 
7. Advantages and Disadvantages of EAN: 
	1. a. Use as air for physiological advantage with air tables or computers. 
	2. b. Use to extend no-decompression bottom times or shorten surface intervals. 
	3. c. Oxygen toxicity hazards and depth limits. 
	4. d. Discussion of myths and facts regarding EAN mixtures. 
8. Procedures: 
	1. a. Use and theory of oxygen analyzer. 
	2. b. Gas analysis and logging. 
	3. c. How to complete and sign a filling station’s EAN fill log, including MOD and oxygen content. 
9. Common Mixing Procedures: 
	1. a. Partial pressure blending. 
	2. b. Continuous blending. 
	3. c. Membrane separation system.


# Skill Performance and Graduation Requirements

1. Use TDI Tables to plan a nitrox dive taking advantage of EAD calculations. 
2. Create a simple written dive plan for a nitrox dive to a maximum operating depth (MOD) calculated with PO₂ between 1.3 and 1.6 depending on environmental conditions. 
3. Demonstrate correct use of oxygen analyzer. 
4. Demonstrate correct cylinder management and labeling in accordance with local practices and/or regulations. 
5. Conduct simple pre-dive briefing (may be simulated if dives are not part of program). 
6. Program nitrox computer, if used, with appropriate oxygen percentage. 
7. Log at least 1 nitrox cylinder analysis to include: MOD and oxygen content.
