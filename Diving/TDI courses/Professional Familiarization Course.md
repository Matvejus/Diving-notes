
# Chapter 1

Technical Diving International (TDI) is the cornerstone of International Training. TDI was founded in 1994, when what we now recognize as “technical diving” was a loosely organized fringe area of diving with little to no formal training available. TDI created certification programs aimed at increasing the safety of adventurous divers who wished to explore beyond the limits of sport diving – including deep depths, overhead environments, and using gas mixtures other than air. TDI is regarded as the leading technical diving training agency.

TDI’s innovative approach to technical scuba training was quickly embraced by scuba instructors who requested similar training materials for use with their sport diving students. This demand led to the creation of Scuba Diving International (SDI) in 1998. SDI caters to the sport diving community and is the largest of the agencies in the International Training family. SDI originally developed entry-level courses but now offers hundreds of specialty courses, professional level certifications and programs geared toward sport divers.

In 2000, International Training recognized the public safety diver training system faced many of the same training gaps that had been present in early technical diving. This put public safety divers at risk of injury or death. As a result, International Training formed Emergency Response Diving International (ERDI) and developed programs that established standardized diving practices for use by first responders and investigators. ERDI’s highly-specialized public safety diver training system offers programs that benefit small volunteer teams and large municipalities alike and has a global reach, with instructors on five continents.

ERDI trains first responders and investigators to utilize standardized diving practices within public safety agencies.

Personal safety has always been International Training’s primary concern. First aid and CPR training was available through SDI. In 2018 we upped our game by launching First Response Training International – the most progressive and technologically advanced CPR and first-aid training agency. Instructors qualified through First Response can prepare their communities to handle first-aid emergencies utilizing cutting-edge training tools and materials. As a member of the International Training family, annual membership is included in standard renewal fees.

First Response Training International is the most progressive and technologically advanced CPR and first-aid agency.

By 2019, International Training ranked as the fastest-growing family of scuba agencies in the world, but one element of the underwater education system was missing: Freediving. International Training merged with Performance Freediving International (PFI) to bring everything for the waterborne professional under one roof. PFI fit perfectly into the innovative atmosphere of International Training. PFI leads the freediving community by providing structured training that uses innovative techniques, training materials, and safety standards.

Online registration is the preferred method for processing certifications. To register a diver online:

- Select ‘Register Users’ from the icons on the left side of the Dashboard. There is [video tutorial](https://youtu.be/6cJnt3KN2eoembed=no&) at the top of this page that can guide you through the process.
- If you are certifying students through a facility, select that facility from your affiliated facilities in the dropdown menu. If you are teaching as an independent instructor, enter a location in the field next to the facility dropdown menu instead. A location may take the form of a geographic location such as ‘City, State’ Province’ or the name of a popular dive site, such as ‘Blue Hole’.
- Next, choose a course and certification date.
- You will then choose whether to have the certification cards and optional wall certificates sent to the student, facility or instructor. Onsite printing is available to facilities that purchase card printers.
- Once the course details and delivery instructions are finalized, you can add students to the course. If your student completed the eLearning, they already have a profile on our website. You can add this profile to the course by entering either the student’s name and date of birth or their email address in the search tool. If your student did not complete the course via eLearning, they can still create a profile on our website prior to certification. We recommend that you ask your students to create their own profiles, as this ensures they know how to log in and navigate the website to view their certifications and update any contact information as necessary. If your student did not complete the course via eLearning and does not have a profile on our website, you can create a profile for them at this stage of the certification process using the information from their student record folder.
- You can add multiple students to the same course. For example, our certification process is designed so you can have a class of several students and only complete the registration process once to certify them. Simply add more student profiles to the course. However, if you are certifying one student for three specialties, you will need to repeat the registration process for each course.
- When all students are added to the course, you will move on to payment details.
- Finally, submit the registration to certify your student. As soon as you submit the registration online, your student will receive an email confirming certification. Their digital certification card as well as a certification verification letter will appear in their profile, and they are all set to dive. The regional representative or World HQ will then print and mail the physical certification card to the designated address.

Note that this registration process is intended for diver or provider-level certifications, and will not work for leadership-level courses, such as SDI Divemaster and above. All leadership-level applications must be submitted to World HQ or the regional representative for review, approval and processing.


# Chapter 3 Risk management

![Liability Release](https://elearning.tdisdi.com/pluginfile.php/536850/mod_lesson/page_contents/3489/Risk_Management.jpg) As mentioned previously, the agencies within the International Training family offer courses designed to provide ease of learning while also promoting safety. Our minimum course standards are plainly written, without unnecessary specificity that can increase legal liability. In the real world, overly rigid training standards limit an instructor’s ability to take environmental conditions and other factors into consideration when conducting training. While we fully expect our members to meet or exceed minimum training standards, we allow them the flexibility to adapt their course structure while doing so.

>Standards are written as minimums to be flexible and defensible. Instructors are expected to craft programs that educate the student while meeting or exceeding the standards.

All International Training instructors are expected to take responsible action to mitigate legal liability. Some examples of risk mitigation methods are: obtaining proper releases, following the standards, ensuring student comprehension before advancing and using certified training assistants. Reference the standards and procedures for specific instructor requirements with releases and records.

Liability and medical releases inform the user of the potential risks associated with the activity. Participants should be instructed to read and sign releases prior to the start of any course. An instructor or potential student should contact the training department if they have questions or concerns regarding a form or standard. Required forms can be found in the forms section of the standards. Good recordkeeping is an important part of liability mitigation. Instructors are expected to obtain and keep signed release forms on file for every student.

> The Training and Membership Services team can assist with questions regarding release forms.

> Instructors are expected to obtain and keep signed release forms on file for their students.

International Training’s Quality Assurance (QA) department goal is to uphold the reputation and high standards of International Training by providing assistance to members who have made mistakes. This ensures that members can be successful and feel empowered to produce the best students possible. The QA department serves members by investigating reported incidents and responding to complaints. A QA investigation involves collecting paperwork and obtaining information about courses to ensure standards are being followed. Any actions taken as a result of a QA investigation are not intended to be punitive. Information about quality assurance and corresponding membership statuses can be found in the corresponding standards section.

It is important to report accidents and incidents to International Training as soon as possible. Even accidents that seem simple can result in litigation and preparing for that litigation needs to take place as soon as possible for all parties involved. Procedures for reporting can be found in the general section of the standards and procedures. Typically, you will need to send digital copies of releases, training records, and dive computer downloads, if applicable. 

Professional liability insurance is required in some areas of the world. If your area has this requirement, International Training must be listed as an additional insured and a copy of the insurance should be emailed to [worldhq@tdisdi.com](mailto:worldhq@tdisdi.com)
